CHAPTER I

The Beginning of the Universe and Life

13.8 billion — 55 million years ago

Time, space, matter, and life all have histories. This chapter follows the long transformation from an early hot universe to a living planet where primates appeared.

13.8 billion years ago

The Big Bang

The universe began expanding from an extremely hot, dense early state and has cooled ever since. It is better understood as a change involving space itself, not as an explosion from one point into pre-existing space.

EnergyWorldview
About 3 minutes after the Big Bang

Nuclei of the Light Elements

The nuclei of mostly hydrogen and helium formed. Neutral atoms appeared only about 380,000 years later; carbon, oxygen, iron, and other heavier elements would be forged much later inside stars and stellar explosions.

EnergyEnvironmentWorldview
About 13.6 billion years ago

The First Stars and Galaxies

After the cosmic dark ages, the first stars ignited. Gravity gathered stars into early galaxies, creating the structures and chemical pathways from which later planets and life could emerge.

EnergyEnvironmentWorldview

4.6 billion years ago

The Birth of Earth

Earth formed along with the rest of the solar system. Over immense spans of time, the young molten planet cooled and developed oceans, a crust, and continents.

EnvironmentEnergy
4.5 billion years ago

The Birth of the Moon

The leading explanation is that a massive body struck the young Earth and the Moon formed from resulting debris. The exact conditions of the impact and the details of lunar formation remain active areas of research.

EnvironmentEnergy
At least 3.5–3.8 billion years ago

Traces of Early Life

Ancient rocks preserve chemical and geological traces that may record early life. When, where, and how life began remains unresolved, and scientists continue to debate how some of the oldest evidence should be interpreted.

BodyEnvironment
About 2.7–2.4 billion years ago

Oxygenic Photosynthesis and the Great Oxidation

After oxygen-producing photosynthesis evolved and spread, atmospheric oxygen rose dramatically around 2.4 billion years ago. The origin of photosynthesis and the oxidation of the atmosphere were connected, but they were not one instantaneous event.

EnergyEnvironmentBody
2.1 billion years ago

The Rise of Eukaryotes

Cells with nuclei appeared. Through endosymbiosis, ancestral cells incorporated the organisms that became mitochondria, opening a path toward more complex forms of life.

BodyEnvironment
About 580 million years ago

Complex Multicellular Life Spreads

Diverse large organisms with frond-like, ribbon-like, and quilted forms spread through Ediacaran seas. They reveal the long ecological prelude that is easily missed when the story begins with the Cambrian Explosion.

BodyEnvironment
Smithsonian source ↗
About 540 million years ago

The Cambrian Explosion

Over several tens of millions of years—brief on a geological scale—the diversity of animal fossils increased greatly. Animal lineages existed earlier, and scientists still examine interacting causes involving oxygen, ecology, and developmental biology.

BodyEnvironment
430 million years ago

Life Moves onto Land

Plants, followed by arthropods, expanded from water onto land. Photosynthetic green life spread across dry ground, beginning the development of terrestrial ecosystems.

BodyEnvironmentMobility
230 million years ago

The Age of Dinosaurs

Dinosaurs diversified through the Mesozoic and became dominant in many terrestrial ecosystems. Mammals were present too, generally occupying smaller ecological roles for much of this era.

BodyEnvironment
66 million years ago

Mass Extinction and the Loss of Non-Avian Dinosaurs

A mass extinction at the K–Pg boundary, driven principally by a major asteroid impact, eliminated many groups including non-avian dinosaurs. Birds survived as one dinosaur lineage, while mammals later diversified into newly opened niches.

EnvironmentBody
55 million years ago

The Rise of Primates

Small primates adapted to life in trees. Depth perception and grasping hands became part of an evolutionary inheritance that would eventually shape the human lineage.
